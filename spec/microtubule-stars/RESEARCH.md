# RESEARCH — How microtubules could be linked to the stars

Five candidate linkages, ordered by seriousness. Each states what
is established, what is speculative, and what would falsify it.

## 1. Quantum gravity (Orch OR) — the serious link

Penrose–Hameroff Orchestrated Objective Reduction: consciousness
is quantum computation in microtubules, terminated by *objective
reduction* — Penrose's proposed gravity-induced wavefunction
collapse. If true, the thing that collapses a superposition inside
a microtubule is spacetime geometry itself: the same physics that
governs stars. The brain would be linked to the stars not by
signal but by sharing the collapse mechanism.

Status: unproven. No direct measurement of the required coherence
in living neurons exists. Falsifier: measuring microtubule
decoherence times far below the OR threshold.

## 2. Warm quantum coherence — the 2024 signal

Babcock et al. (2024) predicted superradiant states in tubulin
tryptophan networks and measured an enhanced fluorescence quantum
yield consistent with the prediction. Craddock et al. (2017)
predicted anesthetics disrupt a 613 THz collective tubulin
oscillation, with disruption tracking clinical potency.

Honesty required: the superradiance signature is an *optical*
effect (~10^15 Hz), not the mechanical coherence Orch OR's
collapse dynamics need — suggestive precedent for warm-biology
coherence, not a demonstration of the mechanism. The 613 THz peak
is a computational prediction awaiting direct observation.
Kerskens' 2022 MRI entanglement signatures are disputed (Warren
2023) and unreplicated. A 2026 Frontiers synthesis concludes:
"a direct link to consciousness is unproven."

## 3. Stellar nucleosynthesis — the material link

Tubulin is carbon chemistry; carbon is forged in stars via the
triple-alpha process. Every atom of every microtubule was made in
a star. Literal, uncontroversial, and the least informative link —
it is true of a rock.

## 4. Asteroseismology — the mathematical link

Stars are studied through their oscillation modes
(helioseismology; Kepler asteroseismology). Microtubule vibrations
are studied as normal modes of a protein lattice. The mathematics
— coupled oscillators, eigenmodes, resonance, phase locking — is
scale-free. This is the frame the accompanying simulation uses:
waves at both ends, different clocks, one formalism.

## 5. Cross-frequency coupling — the physiological motif

Established neuroscience: slow global rhythms modulate fast local
computation (theta–gamma phase–amplitude coupling, Canolty et al.
2006). The simulation extends this motif across scales as a
model: a slow "cosmic" drive modulating a fast neural ring's
coupling gain.

## What the simulation found

Unstructured slow modulation *degrades* holographic recall
monotonically (dwell 0.39 → 0.30 as drive strength 0 → 0.6),
with no 1:1 phase locking across scales (PLV ≈ 0.006) and no
resonance peak. Reading: the brain's cross-frequency coupling
works because it is structured and gated; raw slow drive is
disturbance. In this model, the stars don't help the brain
remember — and that distinction is the finding.

## Honest limits

Classical Kuramoto throughout — no quantum mechanics is
simulated. The "cosmic" drive is a model of slow global
modulation, not stellar data. No claim of cosmic influence on
brains is made or supported. Orch OR remains unproven.
